Capture a Facebook Page A visible Chrome window opens; sign in yourself if Facebook requires it.
A Page URL collects posts from its timeline using the mode below. A single post URL collects that post and its comments only, ignoring the Page's other posts. Personal profiles, groups and other Facebook surfaces are rejected.
Stored in the WARC and capture manifest.
SWM keeps a dedicated Facebook browser profile so your login can persist between captures.
Pinned posts never trigger the lower boundary. SWM stops after five consecutive non-pinned posts older than From.
Posts newer than To must be traversed to reach older material. Their traffic remains in WARC but they are excluded from the normalised CSV and JSONL.
Pinned posts are preserved but do not consume the N-post limit.
Comments are expensive. Facebook paginates comments separately for each post. This can add hundreds of requests, greatly increase runtime and raise the likelihood of throttling or verification.
SWM never asks for your Facebook password. Chrome retains its own session cookies in the dedicated local profile; SWM redacts Cookie/Authorization and Set-Cookie headers plus recognised login/session fields before writing WARC. The browser opens with automatic scrolling paused: log in or resolve verification directly in Chrome, make sure the requested Page is open, then select Start / resume scrolling. WARC capture remains active while scrolling is paused.
Capture Instagram profiles, posts or reels Runs in the background. A browser window opens only if Instagram needs you to sign in or clear a verification.
A profile (username or URL) collects its posts and reels using the mode below. A post or reel URL collects that item only. Explore, hashtag, story and direct-message URLs are refused: they cannot be presented as an archive of anything.
Stored in the capture manifest.
Pinned posts never end the capture on their own. SWM stops after five consecutive non-pinned posts older than From.
Comments are where the rate limit lands. Every post is its own pagination, so a profile of 300 posts with comments on is 300 further request sequences. Expect the run to be paused by Instagram and resumed; it is built for that.
Use a dedicated institutional Instagram account for capture, not a personal one: background collection is the pattern Instagram's automation detection acts on, and it acts on the account. Sign in once in the dedicated Instagram browser profile (the window opens the first time a capture needs it); SWM reads that session when a job starts and holds it in memory only. SWM never asks for the password and never writes the session anywhere else.
