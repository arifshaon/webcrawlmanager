Capture Instagram profiles, posts or reels A Chrome window opens and stays for the run; sign in there if Instagram asks.
A profile (username or URL) collects its posts and reels using the mode below. A post or reel URL collects that item only. Explore, hashtag, story and direct-message URLs are refused: they cannot be presented as an archive of anything.
Stored in the capture manifest.
gallery-dl can be faster than scrolling and reports pinned flags and dates, but Instagram may rate-limit or restrict it; SWM reads its output as it streams, stops it once the capture has what it asked for, and resumes from its cursor after a rate limit. If Instagram refuses the browser's session from gallery-dl while the browser is signed in, the capture lists through the browser instead and records that it did. Post discovery and listing metadata come from gallery-dl and are kept as its output, not as Instagram's responses. Selected media is fetched through the browser where possible; comments are collected from the browser. Needs pip install gallery-dl.
Either way SWM keeps a dedicated Instagram profile: sign in there once. With the browser method the window stays open for the capture, like a Facebook capture.
Pinned posts never end the capture on their own. SWM stops after five consecutive non-pinned posts older than From.
Comments are where the rate limit lands. Every post is its own pagination, so a profile of 300 posts with comments on is 300 further request sequences. Expect the run to be paused by Instagram and resumed; it is built for that.
Use a dedicated institutional Instagram account for capture, not a personal one: background collection is the pattern Instagram's automation detection acts on, and it acts on the account. Sign in once in the dedicated Instagram browser profile (the window opens the first time a capture needs it); SWM reads that session when a job starts and holds it in memory only. Capture YouTube channels, videos or playlists Videos through yt-dlp; the Posts tab through a Chrome window that also handles a sign-in when YouTube asks for one.
A channel (handle or address) collects its videos, Shorts, past live streams and Posts using the mode below. A video address collects that video, its files and comments. A playlist collects its items in order, unavailable entries recorded as such.
Stored in the capture manifest.
SWM keeps a dedicated YouTube profile. The window opens for the Posts tab, and when YouTube asks yt-dlp to sign in; that session is lent to yt-dlp for the run and never written into the package.
Video captures are large. The job holds itself when free space falls below the warning level in Settings, and stops a download mid-file at a third of that level, keeping the partial file to resume.
A channel listing rarely says when a video was published, so each listed item is read before the date rule decides. SWM stops after five consecutive items older than From.
Comments take time. A video with tens of thousands of comments takes hours through yt-dlp. The cap keeps a run bounded; the record says how many YouTube reported and how many were kept.
Use a dedicated institutional Google account for capture, not a personal one. YouTube answers many networks' anonymous requests with a bot check; when it does, the window opens for you to sign in, and SWM lends that session to yt-dlp for the run and deletes the copy at the end. YouTube may restrict an account used this way. Nothing in a capture ever likes, votes, comments or subscribes.
